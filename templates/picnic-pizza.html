An untimed sharing adventure

Pack a fair picnic

Share pizzas, rectangular sandwiches, and fruit bars into equal pieces. Tap pieces to serve them, or choose an answer with a tap, Enter, or Space.

Worked example: sharing a pizza

Cut one pizza into 2 equal pieces. One piece is one half (1/2).

The denominator, the bottom number 2, tells how many equal pieces make the whole. The numerator, the top number 1, tells how many pieces we selected.

Seven levels, from ages 6–7 to 8–9

  1. Fair picnic shares: spot equal-area pieces, then explain why unequal cuts are not equal shares (ages 6–7).
  2. Name the serving: connect selected halves and quarters to words and fraction notation (ages 6–7).
  3. Build halves and quarters: tap pieces to make a serving (ages 6–7).
  4. Try thirds, sixths, and eighths: build portions from smaller equal pieces (ages 8–9).
  5. Compare: look at fractions of the same-size whole (ages 8–9).
  6. Match coverage: see how 1/2, 2/4, and 4/8 cover the same amount (ages 8–9).
  7. Finish the whole: choose the visible missing pieces, without a calculation rule (ages 8–9).

There is no timer, points, or penalty for mistakes. Ask for a hint and try as often as you like.
